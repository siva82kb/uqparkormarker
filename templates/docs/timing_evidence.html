How lateness is measured

We cannot see when a frame was really filmed. The measure rests on two things.

  • Known: a frame was filmed no later than its own timestamp, because a timestamp is never early.
  • Assumed: the camera films at a steady 30 frames a second. Then a frame was filmed at least 1/30 s before the next one, 2/30 s before the one after, and so on.

Example. Frame 100 has the timestamp 10.50 s. Frame 103 has the timestamp 10.52 s.

Frame 103 was filmed by 10.52 s. Frame 100 came three frames earlier, so it was filmed by 10.52 − 0.10 = 10.42 s. Its timestamp says 10.50 s. So that timestamp is at least 0.08 s late.

This gives the least a timestamp can be late. The true lateness can be larger, never smaller. It is used here only to look at the problem. It is not used to change any time.
